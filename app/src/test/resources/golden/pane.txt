  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Large or guided missile (600 m): large, often with wings or control fins.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Aircraft bomb or dispenser (675 m): large, with tail fins (AFVA 32-4022: often 3 to 6 ft long). Dispensers open to release bomblets.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Pile or cache of ordnance (800 m): stacks of ammunition or several items together.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Drone-dropped munitions: often small (grenades or mortar bombs with added tails); use the matching size, or Unknown.

  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_ACTIONS | NUCLEAR DETONATION: WHAT TO DO
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | FEMA: "Get Inside, Stay Inside, Stay Tuned" is the most important protective action, because it reduces fallout exposure.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • GET INSIDE a basement or the middle of a large, dense building. It is best to be in a shelter when fallout arrives. Any shelter is better than being outside for long.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • STAY INSIDE for 12-24 hours, unless given other instructions. Leave earlier only for fire, building collapse or a medical emergency.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • STAY TUNED: AM/FM radio is best; TV, phone or internet if available.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | FEMA gives this message to everyone within 50 miles until fallout areas are identified. Fallout will likely take 10 minutes or more to arrive.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | If you see an intense, unexpected flash, take cover at once, away from windows: the blast wave can follow by seconds.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Caught outside while fallout fell? Once inside, remove the outer layer of clothing and shoes (this can remove up to 90% of radioactive material, FEMA 2024) and brush or wipe off exposed skin and hair.
  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_RINGS | NUCLEAR RINGS: WHAT THEY MEAN
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | The damage zones are FEMA's: Severe, Moderate and Light Damage Zones end about 0.5, 1 and 3 miles from a 10 kT ground-level detonation, and grow about twofold for each tenfold increase in yield (cube-root scaling):
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • 0.1 kt surface: severe 0.11 mi (0.17 km), moderate 0.22 mi (0.35 km), light 0.65 mi (1.0 km).
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • 1 kt surface: severe 0.23 mi (0.37 km), moderate 0.46 mi (0.75 km), light 1.4 mi (2.2 km).
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • 10 kt surface: severe 0.50 mi (0.80 km), moderate 1.0 mi (1.6 km), light 3.0 mi (4.8 km).
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • 100 kt surface: severe 1.1 mi (1.7 km), moderate 2.2 mi (3.5 km), light 6.5 mi (10.4 km).
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • 1 Mt surface: severe 2.3 mi (3.7 km), moderate 4.6 mi (7.5 km), light 13.9 mi (22.4 km).
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Air burst: Blast never asks for a burst height. Each zone is enlarged by the ratio of the farthest ground range of its overpressure (over all burst heights) to the surface range, from Glasstone & Dolan Fig. 3.73c: severe x1.58, moderate x1.67, light x2.0 (the light ratio extends Glasstone's lowest, 1 psi, curve). Burn and radiation rings use the air values with slant range drawn as ground range. Each ring is an outer bound; no single burst produces all of them. Unknown draws the larger of surface and air for every ring, plus fallout.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Burns: 50% chance of second-degree burns to bare skin with a direct view of the fireball, using the most sensitive skin curve of Glasstone Fig. 12.64 (about 3.9 cal/cm² at 10 kt, 5.5 at 1 Mt) and the clear-day (12-mile visibility) ranges of Fig. 7.42. A surface burst delivers about half the heat of an air burst (thermal partition 0.18 vs 0.35).
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Initial radiation: 100 rad (1 Gy) total gamma and neutron dose outdoors without shielding (Glasstone Figs. 8.33 and 8.64; surface bursts corrected by Table 8.37 and §8.65). FEMA uses the same 100 rad line for "radiation injuries". It is received within the first minute.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Fallout: idealized H+1 reference dose-rate contours for a contact surface burst, 100% fission, from Glasstone Table 9.93, scaled for wind speed (§9.96-9.97, valid about 8-45 mph) and drawn downwind of the wind you enter. Real fallout follows the winds aloft, which can differ from the surface wind. Drawn only for surface or unknown bursts.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Accuracy: blast ±20% (Glasstone §5.142), thermal ±25% (§7.85), initial radiation within a factor of 0.5-2 for fission and 0.25-1.5 for thermonuclear weapons (Figs. 8.33a/b).
  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_SHELTER | SHELTER: PROTECTION FACTORS
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | A protection factor (PF) is how many times less radiation you receive inside than outside (LLNL-TR-410067, Table 1):
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Wood-frame house: 2-3. Vehicle: 1.7-2.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • House basement: 10-20.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Apartment building, lower floors: 10; upper floors (core): 100.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Concrete blockhouse: 10-150 (9-inch walls), 30-1,000 (12-inch walls). Underground, 3 ft cover: 5,000.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | LLNL rates shelter Good at PF 100 (core of a large office building, basement of a multistory building), Adequate at PF 10 (periphery or top floor of office buildings, shallow basements) and Inadequate at PF 3 (cars, light single-story buildings without basements). FEMA calls shelter adequate when it cuts the dose tenfold or more.
  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_FALLOUT | FALLOUT AND DOSE RATE
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | For every sevenfold increase in time after the explosion, the dose rate decreases by a factor of ten (Glasstone & Dolan §9.15): 100 R/h at 1 hour is about 10 R/h at 7 hours and 1 R/h at 2 days.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Fraction of the H+1 rate left: H+2 0.44, H+6 0.12, H+7 0.10, H+24 0.022, H+48 0.0096.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | FEMA's radiation zones are set by measurement, never by a model:
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Dangerous Radiation Zone: above 10 R/h outdoors. Can reach tens of miles downwind in the first hours, then shrinks fast.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Hot Zone: 0.01 R/h (10 mR/h) to 10 R/h.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Blast's contours are H+1 reference rates. Fallout reaches distant points hours later, already decayed, so a contour is not the Dangerous Radiation Zone.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Lack of visible fallout does not indicate the absence of fallout; appropriate radiation measurements are required (LLNL NARAC). Fallout at lower levels extends well beyond any drawn zone, particularly downwind.
  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_MIL | MILITARY FALLOUT PREDICTION (ZONES I AND II)
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | The military picture is the simplified fallout prediction of FM 3-3-1 (1994) and FM 3-11.3 (2006, Change 1 2009), the last public US versions. Current manuals (TM 3-11.32, NATO ATP-45) are restricted and may differ.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Zone I: immediate operational concern. Exposed, unprotected personnel may receive 125 cGy or more in a relatively short time (less than 4 hours after the actual arrival of fallout). Casualties and major disruption may occur in parts of this zone.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Zone II: secondary hazard. The total dose to exposed, unprotected personnel is not expected to reach 125 cGy within 4 hours of arrival, but may reach 75 cGy or more within the first 24 hours.
  text 13 TEXT pad=0/3 indent=8 links | • Outside both zones, exposed, unprotected personnel may receive a total dose that does not reach 75 cGy in the first 24 hours after arrival.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Method: Zone I downwind distance from FM 3-3-1 Table E-4 (yield and effective wind speed); Zone II is twice Zone I. Radial lines 20° either side of the effective downwind direction (40° sector) unless the EDM gives a wider angle; tangents from the cloud-radius semicircle of the yield group to the Zone I arc; dashed time-of-arrival arcs every effective wind speed (H+1, H+2, ...).
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Effective downwind message (EDM) lines: 6 digits = downwind direction and speed (090016 = toward 090° at 16 km/h); 9 digits = plus an expanded angle; 3 digits = winds below 8 km/h, the Zone I radius in km, drawn as two circles. Use the line for the yield group (ALFA ≤2 kt, BRAVO ≤5, CHARLIE ≤30, DELTA ≤100, ECHO ≤300, FOXTROT ≤1 MT, GOLF ≤3 MT). EDMs older than 12 hours are not used (FM 3-3-1).
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Arrival: fallout may arrive as early as half the estimated time. If none has arrived by twice the estimate (or 12 hours, whichever is earlier), assume the area will not receive fallout.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | No fallout predictions are made for air bursts over land. Overlapping predictions take the higher zone. The prediction is an estimate only; its boundaries are not dose-rate contours.
  text 13 DIM pad=0/3 links | Table E-4 is used as published, except where it prints a smaller distance for a larger yield or wind (a few cells between 6 and 13 km): there the larger neighbouring value is used. Worked examples reproduced: 16 kt at 16 km/h = 18 km (FM 3-3-1); 35 kt at 29 km/h ≈ 33 km (FM 3-11.3); 100 kt at 16 knots ≈ 30 nmi (FM 3-3-1).
  text 12 bold BLUE pad=10/4 slot=ref_NUC_NBC | NBC 1 NUC OBSERVER REPORT
